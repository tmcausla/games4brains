# 10 Great Games That Will Trap You With "One More Turn"

[< Back Home](/)

There's nothing like a good game that can rope you into a flow state and then make you curse when looking at the clock and realizing it’s 3 in the morning.

“Just one more” is practically the gamer version of famous last words. But the thing that keeps us playing doesn’t always have to be a turn. Sometimes it’s one more loop, one more attempt, one more piece of loot, or one more tile placed exactly where you want it.

I present to you 10 great games that will trap you in their gameplay loops and have you promising yourself that _this one_ will be the last.

## Sid Meier's Civilization VI

![Civilization 6](/images/game-capsules/civ6.jpg)

#### One More Turn

This is the reason the list exists, because it’s the best at what it does in the space of turn-based strategy games.  It is a masterclass of alleviating an overwhelming to-do list by breaking down your turns into a series of single decision points.  A full turn can take 5 seconds or 5 minutes, but the difference is wiped away by how efficiently the game seamlessly presents each pending decision to be made.  By spending less time wondering what _has_ to be done, I get to spend more time deciding _how_ I’m going to defend myself from Gandhi’s approaching army.  

The most incriminating factor is that the game’s UI includes a real-time clock, so you’ve only yourself to blame when you look over and realize you have to wake up for work in 2 hours.    

## Loop Hero

![Loop Hero](/images/game-capsules/loop-hero.jpg)

#### One More Loop

Pushing your luck is one of my favourite game mechanisms.  There is a thin line between playing it safe and risking it all and I love games that allow you to dance along that line.  Loop Hero plays almost like a hands-off RPG where the player is more concerned with only one question: can I do one more loop?  How bad can it be?

Failure isn’t that devastating in Loop Hero thanks to your base camp that is occupied by friendly villagers that will drag your sorry dying corpse back to camp and revive you.  You get to keep a small percentage of your spoils, but you do lose a sizable chunk of treasure if you overstay your adventure.  

But to fight so hard and come so far, surely it’d be a shame to play it safe now.  Surely!

## Midnight Suns

![Midnight Suns](/images/game-capsules/midnight-suns.jpg)

#### One More Battle

The Firaxis team knows how to make superheroes appear powerful.  And then they developed a turn-based RPG card game around these incredible displays of power.  The tactical decisions make you feel clever and the animations make you feel powerful, and then the encounter ends before it wears out its welcome.  The battles are the meat and potatoes of this game but they’re made to be short and punchy (intend your puns, cowards).  So naturally, I try to skim through the Abbey segments so I can get another helping of meat and taters.  

On top of that, there are optional General Missions that let you jump into more battles without having to progress the story.  Now even cutscenes won’t get in the way of grinding through battles in a way that never feels like I’m wasting my time.  

## Celeste

![Celeste](/images/game-capsules/celeste.jpg)

#### One More Attempt

Celeste keeps you hooked because of how elegantly it handles the fail state.  You get a quick death animation, the screen wipes black, and then you’re back into it.  Before you even have a chance to decide if you’re finished playing, the game is already encouraging you to take another crack at it.  

And you do make another attempt.  Because the game emphasises that failure is okay, and that failure is an integral part of success.  Celeste **rarely** feels unfair (_cough_C-sides_cough_), and it goes so far as to provide the player with a feather-breathing exercise to calm their nerves and be ready for the next approach. 

## Picross

![Picross S4](/images/game-capsules/picross.jpg)

#### One More Puzzle

Ah yes, the personal pick.  Something about logic puzzles and filling in grids lights up my brain in spectacular ways.  So it’s the satisfaction of completing a puzzle that compels me forward, because Picross manages to satisfy the logic and the creative parts of my brain when I’m rewarded with this charming pixel art I just built from numbers.  

There have been too many occasions where I decide that this will be the last puzzle, only for my muscle memory to kick in and open a new grid to work on because really _this_ will be the last and then I’m done.  I swear.

## Hades

![Hades](/images/game-capsules/hades.jpg)

#### One More Run

A good roguelite should give the player a reason to try at least one more run, and Hades has several.  Failure is productive because you’re constantly earning resources for persistent upgrades, experimenting with new builds using abilities bestowed by the gods allied to your cause, or learning how to deal with the boss that just whupped you. And if none of that is enough, you can always go pet Cerberus or flirt with Achilles before readying up for another escape attempt. 

Every return reveals the Sisyphean struggle of Zagreus while the player gets a more Promethean experience of bringing back something new. Zagreus is divinely bound to his father’s domain, so success and failure alike will send him back to the House of Hades. Yet the player’s experience keeps moving forward: upgrades become available, relationships develop, and conversations reveal more about the people waiting back home (there’s **so** much dialogue in this game). Despite knowing that Zagreus will end up right back where he started, you press on for the chance to catch a few fleeting moments with his mother, Persephone. 

## Diablo III 

![Diablo 3](/images/game-capsules/diablo3.jpg)

#### One More Loot Drop

As if you needed another reason to battle through the depths of hell, what if you were just a power-hungry loot goblin?  Nearly every quest and every encounter in Diablo III rewards you with some variation of treasure.  However not all treasure is created equal in this game’s loot system.  

Sometimes the goods just aren’t good enough, so you cast off on another quest so you can reap a better reward.  Or the goods are so good that you’re compelled to try out your new build right away (often by completing another quest).  With the huge variations of gear and randomized stat blocks, you’ll keep playing for that next piece that will complement or improve your character.  And then you’ll play just a little more to see what it does.

## XCOM 2

![XCOM 2](/images/game-capsules/xcom2.jpg)

#### One More Action

XCOM 2 puts you in situations so dire that squeezing everything out of your turn is the only way to succeed.  But those actions will create dire consequences themselves.  Maybe you overstepped that flanking position and now there’s a fresh pack of Mutons about to rip through your hapless squad.  Or maybe you whiffed that 95% shot, and there’s no one left to offer overwatch support.  

The game will ensure you don’t rest on your laurels either.  Turn limits, roaming scout units, and invasion forces all force you to weigh whether an action creates an opening or gets you mauled by a Faceless. Every action feels like an opportunity to gain an advantage right up until it becomes the reason your entire squad gets TPK’d. 

## Dorfromantik

![Dorfromantik](/images/game-capsules/dorfromantik.jpg)

#### One More Tile

You won’t feel immense pressure to squeeze another turn out of Dorfromantik.  You probably won’t feel any pressure at all really, since every turn is simply asking you to place one more tile.  The compulsion to keep going doesn’t have to be driven by intense pressure or imminent decisions.  Sometimes it just takes the zen state of mind of wanting to see something grow.

The trick to keep going is by optimizing your placements that score enough points to unlock more tiles to place.  Place a tile, maybe score points, maybe get more tiles to place.  It’s enough to place one more tile because you know the perfect spot for it to go in, but you’ll soon look around at a vast continent with a giant town spanning the map. 

## Helldivers II

![Helldivers 2](/images/game-capsules/helldivers2.jpg)

#### One More Democratic Delivery of Liberty

Online games are wonderfully suited for that “one more” style compulsion.  The sting of defeat can encourage you to go one more round to end on a high note, and the camaraderie of friends will push you into another round to chase another high of success.  Helldivers II makes you stick around for another reason: since you’re already here, why not go above and beyond?

Evacuation becomes available after completing your main objectives, but there’s still more democracy to deliver. Collect some samples. Shut down a few enemy spawn points. Set off a taste of sweet, sweet liberty (read: hellbomb).  Just make sure your squad is privy to the plan, lest a Titan follow you back to the evac site and ruin your day.
