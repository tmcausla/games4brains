# About Me

My path into software development is far from being a straight line.

Over the last decade I've worked in neuroscience, veterinary medicine, restaurant management, and software development. Those careers don't look particularly related on paper, but each one taught me something that I now bring to the way I approach software.

## Neuroscience and Ludology

I studied neuroscience at Carleton University because I was fascinated by how the brain works.

Along the way, I became increasingly interested in games beyond just playing them.  I enjoy understanding _why_ people play them, how games influence behaviour, and how simple rules can produce surprisingly complicated outcomes.

I learned that I could apply this knowledge to increase my understanding of people and systems thinking.  

## Veterinary Technician

I decided to turn my interest in science and my love of animals into a career in veterinary medicine.

I worked as a laboratory animal technician before moving into advanced veterinary imaging and neurosurgery, where I worked as an anesthesia technician.

Veterinary medicine taught me a lot about working carefully in environments where mistakes have real consequences. It also taught me how to communicate clearly when people are stressed, make decisions with incomplete information, and follow established procedures without losing sight of the problem in front of me.

I ultimately left the field after experiencing the burnout and compassion fatigue that came with providing primary care.

It wasn't the end of my interest in science or problem-solving. It was the point where I started looking seriously at what I wanted to do with my life.

## Learning to code

I'd already been teaching myself programming while working in veterinary medicine and during my layoff.

I liked it pretty much immediately.

Programming gave me the same kind of problem-solving I enjoyed in science, but with an important difference: I could build something, change it, break it, figure out why it broke, and try again.

A contact from The VScan introduced me to a game jam, where I got my first opportunity to work on a game with other developers.

That experience convinced me that programming wasn't just something I enjoyed learning about.  

**I wanted to build things with it.** 

## Manager at Chesterfield's Gastro Diner

While I was figuring out how to make that transition, I needed a job.

I ended up at Chesterfield's Gastro Diner and gradually took on more responsibility until I was managing the operation.

Restaurant management isn't software development, but it turned out to reinforce a lot of the same skills I was developing through programming.

I became particularly interested in finding inefficient processes, figuring out why they weren't working, and finding practical ways to improve them.

I also learned a lot about coordinating people, training employees, communicating clearly, and keeping things moving when there are a dozen problems happening at once.

Those skills now follow me into software development. 

## Computer Science Diploma and Software Development

I eventually went back to school and completed a Computer Science diploma at Centennial College.

Since then, I've focused on building software and developing the skills I want to use professionally.

I've built REST APIs, an AI-powered filesystem assistant, a Markdown static-site generator, and several games in Unity. Each project has given me a different way to think about software: architecture, testing, APIs, parsing, AI integration, game systems, and working with unfamiliar code.

I'm now looking to bring those experiences together in a professional software development role.

I want to work in systems that are complicated enough to be interesting, practical enough to matter, and challenging enough that there's something new to learn.
