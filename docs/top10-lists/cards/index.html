10 Great Games That Use Cards

< Back Home

I'm naturally drawn to card mechanics. They have a sense of RNG that seems legible, especially when you know what's possible in the deck. There's a need for resource management and optimizing your turn based on what's available in your hand. They provide a great deal of replayability because every session plays out a little bit differently based on luck of the draw.

I present to you 10 games that are exceptionally good card games or use cards in interesting ways.

Slay the Spire

Given its popularity this is probably the first game that comes to mind when I mention games that use cards. And for good reason: the controls are tight, the strategy goes deep, and you will never play the same game twice (unless you want another crack at that seed). Every character has a strong identity and it's fun to experiment and find out which one you vibe with.

Simply put, no list about card games would be complete without it. Its success spawned a sequel, a board game, and countless imitators. It’s hard to explain exactly why this card game does what it does better than its competitors, but it will likely be the best deck-building roguelite you’ll play for a long time.

Balatro

As if poker wasn’t addictive enough for some people already. Balatro uses your familiarity with poker hands to draw you in and then traps you with its weird atmosphere and Vegas slot machine level of sound design. You’ll delight in the pings and dings as your hand gets scored and you watch the number go biiiiiig.

Balatro has a colourful cast of Jokers that forges a strong identity for itself that makes it more than just another deck-building roguelite.

Inscryption

A game within a game that evolves into three other games before it gains sentience and deletes itself. Honestly a pretty tame premise for what you’d expect a Daniel Mullins production to be. It starts with you playing a gothic animalistic card game where you have to consume your own animals to play the really strong ones. After a few battles the dungeon master tells you to stand up and suddenly the game zooms out and you’re trapped in a small cabin being forced to play this game over and over to earn your freedom.

There’s puzzles hidden around the cabin and the animals on your cards will sometimes provide clues as to what’s really going on. The options for creating cards really encourage you to break the game through splicing, ritual sacrifice, and sometimes pulling teeth to swing the tide of the game. ## Baten Kaitos

The card mechanics help it avoid the classic trap of JRPG’s that rely on stagnant turn-based battles. You have to make quick decisions in battle to build not only your attack combos but also your defense combos on enemies’ turns.

The cards also have a life of their own outside of combat. Some cards will age and change into new cards that behave differently from before. Bananas will ripen into a healing item before rotting into a poisonous attack later on.

Card Shark

Cards in games are usually about abstracting your available abilities and limiting what you can do on a given turn. This game uses a literal deck of playing cards and cheating is the only way you can win. This involves manipulating the deck with quick memorization and sleight of hand or providing signals to your master after pouring wine and taking a cheeky peek at the opponents’ hands.

Make sure you or your master wins without getting caught, because you’re playing against dangerous people that won’t take kindly to being made a fool.

Cosmic Wheel Sisterhood

Tarot cards are a natural story-telling tool, and this game lets you craft your own tarot deck and decide the narrative of your fortune telling. Every decision will shape how others perceive you and the witch that you’ll become.

You’ll provide fortunes for many characters throughout the game and create meaningful stories that you may feel compelled to share with your friends.

Monster Train

A great example of a deck-building roguelite that has a little bit more going on without diluting any of the fun. You get to create a deck by choosing two demonic factions and smashing them together and figuring out how their personalities play off of each other. The cards in your deck are either immediate abilities or units placed in lanes to fend off the angelic invaders.

It’s a hybrid of gameplay that feels good enough that it inspired me to create my own card-based tower defense game.

Death Roads: Tournament

This is a great example of a card game choosing a theme and executing it extremely well. You’ll collect and choose parts for your car and every part provides its own selection of cards for your deck. You’ll play those cards in a series of grid combat style racing; you can maneuver around the road to line up your weapons, you can ram opponents’ cars and cause them to swerve wildly, or you can race ahead of the pack if you’re hurting for the next repair stop.

Weapons are different enough from each other and they behave appropriately to what they are. The customization options are vast and you’re free to prioritize movement or attack power or try and balance the two.

Mega Man Battle Network

This game uses a different parlance for cards: a deck of cards becomes a folder of battle chips in this game, but the randomized availability of options and the way they can combo together is exactly what makes for good card play mechanics.

Even if this is more of a nostalgia pick, it still offers a fresh take on how cards can be played. Most card games involve a static turn-based approach to gameplay, but instead the battle chips you select are the available abilities during a round of real-time combat. You have to balance strong attacks with support chips that change the battlefield to your favour, and selecting certain chips in the right order will unlock extremely powerful abilities that you have to discover through experimentation.

Deck of Haunts

I’d be lying if I said the theme didn’t directly appeal to me. You’re not a hapless investigator exploring a haunted house, you are the haunted house trying to get rid of these pesky intruders. Every night you have to defend the heart of the house by either killing off every human explorer or making them too terrified to explore any further. Between nights you’ll build out the maze of your mansion and design rooms that will terrorize anyone unfortunate enough to wander in.

But beware! The humans will try to escape if they discover a dead body and warn the townsfolk, making future nights more difficult as they become increasingly prepared against you. They may even seek help from the police, or worse, an exorcist.
